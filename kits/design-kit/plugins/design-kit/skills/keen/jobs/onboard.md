# onboard

Designs the first few minutes of a product: what a new person sees before they have any data, and how quickly they get to the first thing worth having.

Interactive at the start, then aggressive. Edits files.

## The idea

A new person arrives with a reason and no patience. The product they see is the empty one: no projects, no history, no numbers. Most products are designed full and shipped empty. This job designs the empty product on purpose, and shortens the path from arriving to the first real result, the moment the person thinks "this works for me". Everything that stands between them and that moment is either removed, postponed or made to earn its place.

## Before you start

Ask once, in one message, with your best guess as the default for each:

- What is the first real result for a new person? One thing they can see or hold, such as "their first invoice sent" or "their own data on the chart".
- What does the product truly need from them before that can happen?

If PRODUCT.md already answers both, do not ask.

## Steps

1. **Walk it as a new person.** Create a fresh account, or use the project's way of starting empty. Go from the first screen to the first real result. Capture every screen at 390 wide. Count the screens, the fields to fill in, and the decisions to make. Time it.
2. **Sort every step and field** on that path: needed for the first result, needed later, or never needed. Be strict. A profile photo, a team name, a phone number and a tour are almost never needed for the first result.
3. **Shorten the path.** Remove what is never needed. Move what is needed later to the moment it is needed. Fill in what the product can work out or sensibly assume, and let the person change it afterwards.
4. **Design each empty screen** on the path using the table below.
5. **Mark the first result.** When it happens, say so plainly and offer the one next thing worth doing. No confetti.
6. **Walk it again**, capture, count and time it, and report on the worksheet with the two walks side by side.

## Each kind of first-time moment

| Moment | What it should do |
|---|---|
| Sign-up | Ask for the least that will work. Every field you remove brings more people through. Say why you need anything unusual |
| An empty list or dashboard | Say what will appear here, show what it looks like when full if that helps, and give one button for the first step. Never a blank area or the word "None" |
| A choice the person cannot judge yet | Pick a sensible default and let them change it later. Do not ask a new person to configure something they have not used |
| A permission (notifications, location, contacts) | Ask at the moment the person does something that needs it, and say what they get. Never on the first screen (Apple's Human Interface Guidelines) |
| Something to learn | Teach it at the moment it is needed, on the real screen, in one line. A tour before use is skipped and forgotten |
| Sample content | Offer it if the product is hard to judge empty. Label it as an example and make it easy to clear |
| Several setup steps | Show how many there are and which are done. Let the person leave and come back without losing anything |
| The first result | Confirm it in plain words and point to one next step |

## Limits

- Never remove something the law, a payment or security requires. If you are unsure whether a field is one of those, keep it and list it.
- Removing a field from sign-up is removing a capability from the product's point of view. List every field you plan to remove and get one yes before you start editing.
- Do not add a tour, a checklist panel or a welcome dialog unless the first walk showed people cannot find the first step without one.
- Everything postponed must still be reachable later. Say where.

## Report

Follow the report shape in SKILL.md. In "What happened", give the three numbers before and after: screens, fields and time to the first result. Under "What you need to do", anything postponed that the person must now be asked for somewhere else. Under Details, the files and each field's fate. Under "Not checked", sign-up routes you could not walk, such as signing in with another service, and email steps you could not receive.
